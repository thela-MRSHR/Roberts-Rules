Robert's Rules Cheat Sheet

Run meetings like a pro. Keep this open on your phone at the board table.

Golden rule: The Living Word Bylaws and the law come first. Robert's Rules fills in the rest. Its 3 jobs: the majority decides, the minority is heard, the meeting keeps moving.

The 6 Steps of Every Motion

  1. 1

    Get the floor

    Raise your hand. Speak only after the Chair recognizes you.

  2. 2

    Make the motion

    State the exact action. One idea per motion.

    "I move that..."
  3. 3

    Get a second

    No second, no motion. A second means "let's discuss it," not "I agree."

    "Second."
  4. 4

    Chair states it

    The Chair repeats it word for word. Now it belongs to the board.

    "It has been moved and seconded that..."
  5. 5

    Discuss

    Maker speaks first. Talk through the Chair. Nobody speaks twice until everyone has spoken once.

  6. 6

    Vote and announce

    Voice, hands, or roll call. The Chair announces yes, no, abstain, and whether it passes or fails. The Secretary records it.

Memory hookMove it. Second it. State it. Debate it. Vote it. Announce it.

Say This

Make a motion"Madam/Mr. Chair, I move that the board approve [action] for [$] for [purpose]."
Adopt a resolution"I move that the board adopt Resolution [number], approving [action]."
Amend"I move to amend the motion by adding [words] after [words]."
Friendly amendment"Would the maker accept adding [words]?" If anyone objects, it needs a second and a vote.
Ask a question"Point of information. How much is left in our budget line?"
Flag a rule problem"Point of order. This item is not on the agenda."
Recuse"I am recusing myself from this item due to a conflict of interest." Say it before discussion, then step away.
State the motion"It has been moved and seconded that [motion]. Is there discussion?"
Keep order"Please address your comments through the Chair." / "Let's hear from members who have not spoken yet."
Go to executive session"We will now move into executive session for [personnel / confidential] matters. Guests, thank you; we will invite you back."
Call the vote"Seeing no further discussion, the motion is [restate]. All in favor, say aye. Opposed? Abstaining?"
Announce"With [X] yes, [Y] no, and [Z] abstaining, the motion carries / fails."
No second"Hearing no second, the motion is not considered."
Rule on a point of order"The point is well taken" or "not well taken," plus one sentence on why.
4

Voting & Quorum

Quorum

Minimum seated members needed to act. No quorum: discuss, but no votes.

Majority

More than half of votes cast, unless bylaws say "of the board" or "of those present."

Two-thirds

For anything that limits members' rights: closing or limiting debate, suspending rules.

Ties fail

A tie means the motion does not pass.

Roll call

Each member's vote is recorded by name in the minutes.

Check the Living Word Bylaws

Quorum number, how abstentions count, and when the Chair votes.

5

Church Board Guardrails

These come before Robert's Rules. Every time.

  • Bylaws firstRobert's Rules never overrides the Living Word Bylaws or the law. You can suspend Robert's Rules, never the Bylaws.
  • Conflicts out loudDisclose any personal or family financial interest, then step out of the discussion and the vote.
  • Two-person money ruleNo one handles church money alone. Two people count, two signatures approve large payments.
  • Child safety firstAnyone serving minors, including CBAC staff and volunteers, is screened and never alone with a child.
  • Decisions in writingEvery vote goes in the minutes. Major decisions get a signed, numbered resolution.
  • No casual email votesActing without a meeting needs every director's written consent, and only if the Bylaws allow it.

Oops & Fixes

Discussing before a motion
Chair asks, "Is there a motion?" Then discuss.
"I so move" with no details
State the full action. The Secretary needs exact words.
Two ideas in one motion
"I move to divide the question."
Shouting "Question!" to end debate
Say "I move the previous question." Needs a second and 2/3.
Using "table" to kill a motion
Move to postpone indefinitely, or vote it down.
A director with a conflict votes
Disclose, recuse, step out. The Secretary notes it in the minutes.
Voting by email between meetings
Only with every director's written consent, if the Bylaws allow. Otherwise, wait for the meeting.
